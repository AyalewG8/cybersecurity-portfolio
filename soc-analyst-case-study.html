Challenge

Separate meaningful indicators from normal activity.

The synthetic timeline mixes suspicious authentication, endpoint, and network activity with benign policy, backup, inventory, and update events. The task is to reach a defensible conclusion without overclaiming what the evidence proves.

My Role

Act as the first-line analyst.

I designed the scenarios, defined evaluation logic, correlated the evidence chain, selected severity and containment decisions, and built a structured note that records indicators, disposition, and scope.

Analyst Workflow

Repeatable alert-to-incident methodology

Validate

Confirm the alert, affected identity, endpoint, and time window.

Correlate

Join authentication, EDR, network, and file activity into one timeline.

Scope

Separate supported indicators from benign events and assumptions.

Contain

Isolate the endpoint, contain the account, preserve evidence, and escalate.

Document

Record severity, evidence, decision, disposition, and limitations.

Decision

Suspected endpoint compromise with lateral movement

Recommended first action: Isolate ws-044, contain the jsmith account, preserve relevant evidence, and escalate under the incident-response process.

Analytical restraint: The timeline does not establish data exfiltration. The conclusion stays within the evidence.

Outcomes

What the finished project demonstrates

Better signal handling

The investigation requires flagging five suspicious events while limiting false positives from routine activity.

Actionable containment

The response connects technical evidence to specific endpoint, account, evidence-preservation, and escalation actions.

Consistent documentation

The triage simulator generates a local text note containing the alert ID, indicators, analyst decision, recommended disposition, and scope statement.

Defensible conclusions

The model rewards evidence-based scoping and explicitly distinguishes confirmed observations from unsupported assumptions.
